MSU MANKATO OFF-CAMPUS HOUSING SURVIVAL GUIDE
A student-written guide to not getting burned when you move off campus

== WHEN TO START LOOKING ==

The biggest mistake freshmen make is waiting until summer to look for fall housing. The good off-campus places near Minnesota State University, Mankato lease up FAST. The nicer complexes like The Hub on 7th and Maple Ridge often have their best units signed by January or February for the following August. If you wait until May, you're picking from leftovers. General rule: start touring in October-November of the year before you want to move in, and be ready to sign by late January if you find something good. Group houses through places like the bigger landlords go even earlier because friend groups lock them down a year ahead.

== HOW LEASES WORK ==

Most off-campus leases in Mankato are 12-month leases that run August 1 to July 31. This trips up a lot of students because you pay rent through the summer even if you go home in May. A few complexes offer 9-month or academic-year leases, but they charge more per month to make up for it. Oakwood Townhomes is a classic example of the 12-month trap — great townhomes for groups, but no summer sublet option, so you eat the summer rent whether you're there or not.

Read the lease before you sign. Specifically look for: the lease term (9 vs 12 month), whether utilities are included, the pet policy and fees, the subletting policy, and the move-out cleaning requirements. If you don't understand a clause, ask the leasing office to explain it in writing.

== DEPOSITS AND HOW TO PROTECT YOURS ==

Security deposits in Mankato typically run one month's rent, though some places do a flat $300-500. Minnesota law requires landlords to return your deposit within 21 days of move-out, along with an itemized list of any deductions. They cannot legally keep it for "normal wear and tear" — only for actual damage or unpaid rent.

The single most important thing you can do: TAKE TIMESTAMPED PHOTOS of every room the day you move in, before you put anything down. Photograph existing carpet stains, wall scuffs, appliance dents, everything. Students at Oakwood Townhomes have reported losing chunks of their deposit (one person lost $400) over carpet damage that was there before they moved in — and without move-in photos, it's your word against theirs. Email the photos to yourself the day you move in so they're timestamped.

When you move out, take the same photos again. Get the place professionally cleaned if the lease requires it and keep the receipt. At Maple Ridge it's common to lose around $50 for a flat "cleaning fee" even if you clean yourself, so budget for small deductions like that.

== UTILITIES ==

Some Mankato complexes include utilities (heat, water, trash, sometimes internet) in the rent, and some make you set everything up yourself. This matters more than people realize. Riverside Commons includes utilities, which is part of why its ~$450/month rent is such a good deal — your actual monthly cost barely moves in winter. Compare that to a place where you pay your own heat: a poorly insulated older unit can cost you $150+ a month in heating during a Minnesota January. Cedar Pointe is the cautionary tale here — cheap rent (~$475) but you pay your own heat, and residents report the older units struggle to stay above 62 degrees in deep winter no matter what the thermostat says.

Always ask: "Are utilities included? If not, what did the previous tenant pay per month in winter?" A good leasing office will tell you.

== PARKING ==

Parking ranges from "free and plentiful" to "actively hostile" depending on the complex. The places closest to campus tend to have the worst parking because land is tight. Stonebrook Flats is right next to campus — amazing for walking to class — but the lot is small and they tow aggressively; multiple students have been towed twice. Cedar Pointe is far from campus (a 20-minute drive, you NEED a car) but parking there is free and easy. If you don't have a car, prioritize walkability and bus access over parking. If you do have a car, ask how many permits come with the unit and whether guests can park.

== ROOMMATES ==

Most of the affordable options near MSU are multi-bedroom units meant for roommate groups — 3, 4, and 5 bedroom layouts are common. Splitting a 4-bedroom dramatically lowers per-person rent, which is why so many students go this route. Before you sign with roommates, agree IN WRITING on how you'll split rent, utilities, and what happens if someone moves out mid-lease (many leases make all roommates "jointly liable," meaning if one person bails, the rest owe their share). Pick roommates on reliability, not just friendship.

== RED FLAGS BEFORE YOU SIGN ==

- The leasing office is vague or won't put answers in writing.
- Slow maintenance reputation. Ask current residents how long repairs take. At Maple Ridge, an AC outage took 9 days to fix in August — ask about response times before you sign, not after.
- Thin walls. Tour during the day AND ask about noise. Several complexes near campus have paper-thin walls; weekend noise at downtown spots like The Hub on 7th is intense.
- Pest history. Older, cheaper buildings sometimes have roach or mouse issues. Riverside Commons had a roach problem, though the landlord did pay for pest control twice.
- Hidden fees. Pet rent, parking permits, "amenity fees," and trash fees can add $100+/month on top of base rent. Birchwood charges a $300 pet deposit PLUS $35/month pet rent per animal.
- Flooding risk. Units near the river can flood. Riverside Commons' lot flooded twice one spring and damaged residents' cars — park on the high end of the lot.

== BOTTOM LINE ==

Cheap rent is not the same as cheap living. Factor in utilities, parking, pet fees, and the cost of slow maintenance before you decide. The best value is usually a mid-priced place with utilities included, decent management, and a location you can actually walk or bus from. Tour in person, talk to current residents, take photos, and never sign anything you haven't read.
