FORUM MEGATHREAD: "Honest off-campus housing advice for incoming MSU students?"
[Anonymous student forum — names removed]

ORIGINAL POST:
Incoming transfer here, never lived off campus before. I keep seeing the same complexes mentioned but I want the REAL talk. What's actually worth it, what's a trap, and what do you wish someone told you before you signed? Go.

REPLY 1:
The thing nobody tells you: a 12-month lease means summer rent even when you're not here. I signed at Oakwood Townhomes with three friends, loved the place — attached garage, super quiet, felt like a real house — but we paid all summer while we were home. Do the math on 12 months, not 9, when you compare prices. Also TAKE MOVE-IN PHOTOS. They kept $400 of our deposit for "carpet damage" that was 100% there when we moved in and we had nothing to prove it.

REPLY 2:
Cheapest legit option is Riverside Commons, hands down. Like $450 a month and utilities are INCLUDED, which matters way more than people think in a Minnesota winter. Yeah the building's old and I had roaches my first semester, not gonna lie. But the landlord actually paid for pest control twice and came out himself when my heat died in December. One warning: it's by the river and the lot flooded twice last spring, a couple cars got water damage. Park uphill.

REPLY 3:
Counterpoint to everyone saying go cheap: I lived at Cedar Pointe for the rent ($475) and regretted it. It's a 20 minute DRIVE from campus so you 100% need a car, and the heating in the older units is a joke — my place would not get above 62 in January and maintenance just said "that's normal." Parking's free at least, but there were two break-ins in the lot last winter and they barely plow after snow. You get what you pay for.

REPLY 4:
If you have money and want nice, The Hub on 7th downtown is the move. Brand new, everything works, study lounges open 24/7 that saved me during finals. BUT it's ~$1,150 for a one bedroom and the weekends are LOUD because it's right in the bar district. Elevator's also broken half the time and I'm on the 5th floor. Had a package stolen from the lobby once too even though there's a "front desk." Worth it for the location IMO but know what you're signing up for.

REPLY 5:
Honestly for most people the answer is something mid-range. I'm at Maple Ridge, ~$600 for my room in a 3-bed, gym and pool are actually nice, quick drive to campus. Downsides: walls are thin (I hear my neighbor's TV every night) and maintenance is SLOW — my AC went out in August and took 9 days. But it's a solid middle option, not too expensive, not a dump.

REPLY 6:
Pet owners listen up: Birchwood Student Living is the only place I found that's truly pet friendly — they have an actual dog park on site and a bus stop right outside that goes to campus, which is clutch if you don't have a car. The catch is the pet fees: $300 deposit plus $35 a MONTH in pet rent per animal. Two pets = $70/month on top of rent. Staff are super nice though, they know your name.

REPLY 7:
Re: parking — if you bring a car, DO NOT live at Stonebrook Flats no matter how nice it is to walk to class. The lot is tiny and they tow constantly, I got towed twice in one semester. Great location, in-unit washer/dryer is amazing, but the parking situation made me move out. Also found mold in my bathroom ceiling the first month and they tried to blame me for "not running the fan." Took three emails to get it cleaned.

REPLY 8:
Summary for lazy people scrolling:
- Cheapest + utilities included: Riverside Commons (old, watch flooding)
- Nicest + most expensive: The Hub on 7th (loud, broken elevator)
- Best balance: Maple Ridge (thin walls, slow maintenance)
- Best for groups: Oakwood (12-mo lease, take deposit photos)
- Best for pets: Birchwood (pricey pet fees)
- Walkable but bad parking: Stonebrook (towing, had mold)
- Cheap but far + cold: Cedar Pointe (need a car, heating issues)
Whatever you pick: read the lease, take move-in photos, and ask if utilities are included. Good luck.
